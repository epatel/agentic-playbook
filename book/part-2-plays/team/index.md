# Team

Somebody above you has decided that the team will use these tools. What arrived was a licence and an
expectation; what did not was any description of how nine people use them without producing nine
incompatible ways of working. So the team invents one, in private, and discovers where the
versions disagree during code review — a slow and public place to find out that half the team
thinks the agent's plan belongs in the pull request and half think it is noise.

DORA's 2025 survey puts the mechanism plainly: AI's primary role is "as an amplifier — it magnifies
the strengths of high-performing organizations and the dysfunctions of struggling ones". A team with
clear norms gets more of them; a team without gets more of what it had.

The suite is one idea: a team's practice with these tools is an artefact, and it has to be written,
maintained, and handed over like any other. What is not written down is not a practice. It is a
habit several people happen to share, and a shared habit does not survive a model release or a new
joiner.

Three plays, the same idea at three layers.

[*Build the working agreement*](build-the-working-agreement.md) — the decisions. Which choices have
consequences for other people and so have to be common, which stay nobody's business but yours, and
how to write the result so that amending it beats ignoring it.

[*Collect and refine as a team*](collect-and-refine-as-a-team.md) — the material. Turning what
individuals worked out alone into shared skills and shared context, including the half nobody does:
taking things back out.

[*Onboard someone into all this*](onboard-someone-into-all-this.md) — the transfer. Getting the
tenth person productive on a surface that did not exist two years ago, and using them as the only
free audit of it you get.
