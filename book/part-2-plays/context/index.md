# Context

Everything else in this book is downstream of what the agent could see. The model is the one part of
the stack you cannot change this afternoon; the context is the part you control completely, and
almost nobody treats it as a design surface — it fills up the way a garage fills up, one reasonable
decision at a time, none of them ever reversed.

The suite is one idea: signal over noise. Not less context as a virtue in itself, but a higher
proportion of what the agent reads changing what it does. The noise is rarely wrong — it is true,
once useful, and nearby, which is what makes it hard to throw out. Every line competes for attention
with every other line, including the twelve that mattered.

Three plays, the same move at three layers.

[*Write the brief the agent actually reads*](write-the-brief-the-agent-actually-reads.md) — signal
in the layer that loads every session. The file every project now has and few projects maintain:
what belongs in it, what is quietly making it worse, and how to tell without reading four hundred
lines end to end.

[*Starve the context*](starve-the-context.md) — signal in a single run. Reduction never happens by
accident, a fuller context often produces a worse answer, and a tool reporting how much it saved you
is a different artefact from your bill.

[*Scope a task to fit the window*](scope-a-task-to-fit-the-window.md) — signal in the task itself.
How much can be handed over at once, how to recognise a job that has outgrown its session, and how
to hand state to the next one.

None of the three needs a budget line, a procurement cycle, or anyone's approval. They mostly need
deleting things, which is the harder of the two skills.
