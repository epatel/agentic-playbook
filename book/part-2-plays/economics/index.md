# Economics

Nobody has an intuition for this line item yet, and the reason is structural rather than a failure
of attention. It is the first cost most engineering teams have carried that moves with how a
developer phrases a sentence, how long they leave a window open, and whether they went to lunch.
So it gets managed the way any unfamiliar number gets managed: somebody posts the invoice in the
team channel, everyone resolves to be careful, and the resolution outlives the sprint by about a
week. The part everyone watches is the price per token, which is close to the least important
thing on the bill.

The suite is one idea: cost is a product, not a price. What a piece of work costs is the price per
token, multiplied by the tokens it consumes, multiplied by how many attempts it takes to get
something you would ship. The last two factors are where the money is. The first is the only one a
vendor publishes, and the only one you do not control.

Three plays, the same idea at three layers.

[*Understand what you are paying for*](understand-what-you-are-paying-for.md) — the session. Why
the conversation rather than the answer is the expensive part, what caching buys and what breaks
it, and how to read a bill instead of a dashboard.

[*Match the model to the job*](match-the-model-to-the-job.md) — the model. Which work needs the
frontier tier, what a cheap model costs when it takes three times as many turns, and how to settle
that on your own workload rather than on a price list.

[*Know when not to use an agent*](know-when-not-to-use-an-agent.md) — the task. The jobs where
reaching for an agent loses on time, money, or correctness, and the tests that identify them before
you have spent the afternoon finding out.
